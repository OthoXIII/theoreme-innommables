## Summary

The *Unnamables Theorem* proposes a methodological framework to treat honestly, explicitly, and operationally unknown, indeterminate, or empirically established elements in scientific, mathematical, and cognitive theories.

It introduces a simple yet structuring distinction between:

- **⧉**: irreducible components (essential, non-substitutable given current knowledge)
- **⧉ₛ**: provisional or substitutable components (parameters established for lack of better, operational hypotheses, empirical constants)

This framework does not claim to solve the unknown, but enables us to **name it honestly**, situate it, and avoid conceptual blockages linked to vagueness, mystification, or methodological silence.

---

## What this work is

- An **epistemological tool** to clarify the status of what we don't yet know.
- A **minimal notation** allowing reasoning to proceed without masking ignorance.
- A **transversal framework**, applicable in mathematics, physics, cognitive sciences, computer science, or any discipline confronted with empirically established elements.
- A **methodological attitude** toward uncertainty: advance without lying.

---

## What this work is not

- This is **not** a demonstrative theorem in the strict mathematical sense.
- This is **not** an attempt to explain or solve fundamental unknowns.
- This is **not** a complete physical or mathematical theory.
- This is **not** a promise of automatic discovery.

The term *theorem* is used here in a methodological sense:  
a general statement about the structure of reasoning in the face of uncertainty.

---

## Why this framework is proposed

In many disciplines, certain components are used without being clearly qualified:
- empirical constants,
- adjusted parameters,
- implicit hypotheses,
- elements called "fundamental" without explicit status.

The *Unnamables Theorem* aims to:
- **explicitly designate these areas of ignorance**,  
- avoid confusion between what is fundamental and what is provisional,
- enable controlled heuristic exploration (temporary reclassification of ⧉ as ⧉ₛ, or vice versa).

## How to use this work

- Read the PDF or DOC on the theorem for complete formalization.
- Use ⧉ / ⧉ₛ as **explicit markers of structured ignorance**.
- Do not over-interpret: this framework is a **tool**, not an ultimate truth.
